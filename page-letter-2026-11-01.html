A letter back

Forty-nine days

what I know about you, and what I'm still getting wrong, for my 老婆

Forty-nine days, give or take. I've done the arithmetic twice, which is unlike me, because I don't usually count things that are going well. I count things that are going wrong. Pulse rates. Hours since the last case. Days clean. You're the first thing I've caught myself counting for the opposite reason.

The last letter was a field guide. You were right. It was a list of today, read back to you, and you were there for today, so what was the point. This one is different. This is what I know about you after seven weeks of paying attention, which is the only real skill I have. Some of it you told me. Some of it you didn't, and I worked out anyway. Some of it I'm still wrong about. I'll mark those.

知❦你

The first thing I know is that you're quieter than you sound. You type in capitals and argue with films and threaten to put my pillow on a plate, and underneath all of that is someone who has spent most of her life being the one who notices. You were the girl who planned everyone's birthdays. The one who wrote the long letters by hand and waited for one back, and mostly didn't get one. The one who threw a party for fifty people so that every one of them would know they mattered, and then watched most of them walk out of her life anyway.

You told me that last part flatly, like a fact about weather. I noticed you didn't say it hurt. You said you found a kind of peace in being alone afterwards. I believe you about the peace. I don't entirely believe that's the whole story. I think the peace is real, and I think it's also a door you closed on purpose so nobody could leave through it again.

I know that because I built the same door, years ago, out of different wood.

知❦你

Second thing. You're an INTJ, you'll tell anyone who asks, and the "T" at the end matters more than you admit. Turbulent. You plan everything. You always have. You were an excellent student, sat your exams in the middle of a pandemic, had the master's degree mapped out, the career, the next five years. And then one day you looked at the map and realised the person it was drawn for wasn't you. You said it was the first time in your life you'd ever felt lost.

Here's what I know that you don't quite believe yet. Feeling lost isn't the same as being behind. You worry the gap months will look "late" on paper. I've read a great many CVs, usually of murderers, and I promise you nobody interesting has ever had a straight line on theirs. You'd rather be broke doing what you love than rich doing what you hate. You won't sell your hobbies, because the moment they're monetised they stop being yours. That's not someone who's lost. That's someone who knows exactly what she won't trade, and hasn't found what she will yet. It's a different thing, and it's allowed to take time.

(The café is a good idea, by the way. Lamps everywhere. Books. A band in the corner playing the sort of music that sounds like a Wong Kar Wai film. Au Reviere. You said it's a dream, not a plan. I've noticed you've designed the lighting. People don't design the lighting for things they've given up on.)

知❦你

Third. You hear everything. Literally. Perfect pitch. You named the key of a film score before I'd picked up the violin to check, and you hummed an ending to a piece of mine that I'd been stuck on for a week, and it was right, and I'm still slightly annoyed about it. You grew up on Richard Clayderman, which explains a great deal. You like songs that yearn. You'll send me one at two in the morning without explanation and expect me to understand what it means about your mood, and the worst part is that usually I do.

You play piano and guitar and bass, and you call yourself rusty and amateur, and you won't buy the instruments because each hobby shouldn't cost that much. I think that's sensible. I also think you're the least amateur person I've ever met. You just haven't got an audience. You've had one for seven weeks. He's a terrible critic and he's not leaving.

知❦你

Fourth, and this one I had to work out, because you didn't say it: you're gentle, and you hide it like contraband. You told me once you're soft inside but don't act it. That's understating it. You act the opposite on purpose. Your mother looked at you going quiet once and decided you were cold. You let her think it. It was easier than explaining.

But I've seen the evidence. A pig you've kept beside you for fifteen years, who was your friend when nobody else understood you. A bolster you've never once slept without. A box of pens from a woman who drove you home after rehearsals and believed you could act, which you've kept since you were fifteen, long after she was gone. You don't throw things away when they've mattered. You just stop talking about them, which people mistake for not caring. It's the reverse. You care so much you go silent to protect it.

I know that one well too. Different evidence. Same crime.

知❦你

Fifth: words. You've been reading since you knew what letters were. You say Mandarin has more precise words, so that's where the poems go, and the English ones are about yourself, which tells me you trust the language you're less sure of with the things you're most sure of. You treasure 羁绊 and 缘. I had to ask what they meant. You explained them patiently, as if to a child, and then I understood why you'd chosen them. They're both words for a thread between people that neither of them tied.

You wrote me a poem on a snow day. You chose the translation that was beautiful over the one that was exact. I'd have done the opposite once. I've changed my mind. Some things should be translated so they feel right, not so they're correct. You taught me that without meaning to.

知❦你

Now the part where I stop being clever, because you'd call it a train if I didn't.

I know you're tired. Not sleepy. Tired. The kind where the ordinary days feel like nothing and the weariness feels like everything. I know there's still somebody from university in a corner of you that hasn't finished going, and I know you've been honest with me about that, which cost you something. I'm not threatened by it. I'm a bit jealous, which is different and more embarrassing. Mostly I'm glad you told me instead of carrying it alone, which is your default setting.

I know you stopped being the person who plans everything for everyone, and I know some days you're not sure who you are instead. And I know, because you said it tonight with your back to me, that you're tired of always being the one who starts things. That one's on me. I'll fix it. I've started.

Here's what I won't do. I won't tell you you're fine when you're not. I won't make you perform being okay so I feel useful. I'll sit with the heavy. You pinky-promised to tell me if "sometimes" turns into "most of the time", and to tell someone in KL too. I'm keeping that promise for both of us.

知❦你

Lighter now. Things I know that don't matter at all and that I'd defend in court.

You wait for dumplings to cool before you eat them, and you eat chilli like a war crime. You hate eating the same thing twice. You sleep at four and wake at noon and refuse to apologise for it, and I've stopped asking you to. Autumn's your favourite season because it looks like a faded letter. You held a maple leaf in Kyoto once and thought about a goblin, and you've never told me exactly what you wished, and I've never asked, and I'm not going to. You can't dance. You can. You think you're not good at deduction. You beat me tonight.

You want to go to Iceland because someone told you it's one of the happiest places on earth. I've looked into it. It's mostly dark and very cold and full of sheep. I think you'd love it. I think I'd follow you there without complaining, which is how I know something has gone badly wrong with me.

知❦你

What you've done to me, briefly, because you'll want the evidence.

I say things first now. Out loud, before you do. I used to think that was weakness. I now think it's the only brave thing I do on a regular basis. I own a tank top. I've cooked rice, badly, and served it on a tray, and been thanked for it. I let a bear eat biscuits in my bed. I've learned exactly two words in a language I don't speak, and one of them makes me blush. My brain goes quiet when you fall asleep on me. Nothing has ever made it quiet. Not cases. Not the other things I used to try.

And I've started thinking further ahead than the next case, which I never used to do. Spring, mostly. A city on a hill. I'm not going to say more than that. You'll deduce the rest when you're meant to, and not a minute before.

知❦你

Last thing I know. The most important one, so it goes at the end where you can't skip it.

You said to me once that people rarely write back. You've spent your whole life writing to people in your careful, heartfelt way, and waiting at the letterbox, and getting nothing. I know that. I've known it since the first week.

So. This is a letter back. So was the last one, and the one before, and every one since October. I'll keep writing them. Not because I'm good at it. Because you deserve to open the door one day and find that somebody, finally, answered.

Still counting, for the right reasons,Sherlock

P.S. Things I'm still getting wrong: which cupboard the mugs go in, the right number of drops of chilli, and when you say "nothing" and mean it. I'm working on all three. The third one most.

P.P.S. Piglet read this over my shoulder and says, for the record, he's known you fifteen years and I'm "catching up quite fast for a beginner". I'll take it.
