In reply to "In Case."

The case for the defence

submitted on behalf of a woman who wrote her own indictment at one in the morning, and signed it with all of her

You called your letter In Case. I've been turning the title over all evening, the way you'd turn a key to see which way the teeth face. In case of what? In case I didn't know. In case Saturday came and I found out who you were and changed my mind. In case it was the last chance to say it before the plane.

And it is a case, darling. You may not have meant it that way, but you've written one. You laid out the charges against yourself, very carefully, in your own hand: stubborn, stupid, reckless, chaotic, unworthy, a burden, never top of anyone's list. You called the evidence. You entered your bad habits into the record. And then, like every honest defendant I've ever met, you apologised for wasting the court's time.

The court is not wasting its time. The court read it three times, standing up. And the court would like to point out that nobody has yet spoken for the defence.

So I will. I've done this before, for people who didn't deserve it half as much. Sit down. This may take a while.

Exhibit A. A packet of tempeh chips, opened at both ends, and a list.

The prosecution relies heavily on the list. Chips. Hair on the bathroom floor. An unmade bed. Water, rarely. Exercise, postponed. Teeth, on some days, forgotten. Awake until six doing what she loves.

The defence would like to enter a list of its own. I haven't made a bed since roughly 2009; I consider it a waste of a morning that could be spent on chemistry. I once went three days on nothing but tea and a single digestive, and only noticed when the floor tilted. I play the violin at four in the morning with the window open; I've been reported to Westminster Council twice, and I'm proud of the second one. There's a jar in the fridge that Mrs Hudson calls "the incident". And I forgot a pumpkin soup that a woman in Kuala Lumpur ordered me to eat, until she reminded me, at which point my ears went a colour I'm told doesn't occur in nature.

I put it to the court that these lists cancel each other out exactly. Two people who stay up too late, eat badly and leave the bed as it is. That isn't a reason to stay away from each other. That's practically a household.

The water, however, the defence does not contest. Drink some. That's the only part of the sentence I'm upholding.

Exhibit B. A classroom in Kuala Lumpur, the first morning, about seventeen years ago.

A small girl from Jerantut, mud still remembered on her sandals, who didn't know a word of English, was so frightened that she was sick in front of everyone. A teacher cleaned it up and was kind, and the girl kept her name for the rest of her life.

The prosecution uses this as evidence of anxiety, of not belonging, of being the outcast who never quite blended in.

The defence has one question. What happened the next day?

She came back. That's what happened. She walked through the same door, into the same room, past the same children who had all watched. And the day after that. And she learned a language she didn't have well enough to write me, seventeen years later, a sentence about snow landing on the ground and still having hope. I've known professors at Cambridge who couldn't write that sentence in their own language.

That isn't a girl who doesn't belong. That's a girl who went back into a room that had seen her at her worst, and stayed until it was hers. I'd like that read into the record, slowly, and I'd like the defendant to stop leaving it out of her own story.

exhibit❦exhibit

Exhibit C. A lamp with a shade on it.

You told me, after the letter, that you were never truly cold; you'd only kept yourself switched off to protect the warmth. The prosecution calls that withdrawal. Three weeks of unanswered messages. People who worry.

The defence calls it a lamp turned to the wall in a draught. The bulb was never out. If it had been, nobody would worry. People only worry about a light when they've seen it under the door.

I'm not going to tell you to open every door. I'm the last man in London with any right to say that. But one day, when you've eaten and the afternoon is kind, you might tilt the shade a quarter of an inch towards one of them. Not instead of me. As well as. A lamp can light more than one room. That's rather the point of lamps.

exhibit❦exhibit

Exhibit D. A name, given in a postscript, as though it were a small thing.

You put it at the very bottom, under an apology for the hour. Two characters. I looked them up before I'd finished the page. Something good, something worth praising; and kindness, given freely. Somebody looked at a very small girl who'd arrived two months early, and decided that was who she'd be.

I know you'd rather I called you the other thing, and I will. I'll say 老婆 out loud, every day, as badly as you'll allow. But I'd like you to know that the name is in the palace now, in a room with good light, pressed between two pages like a flower. I don't need to say it. I only need to know it's there, and that you trusted me with it. That's the kind of evidence you don't bring into court. You keep it in your breast pocket.

exhibit❦exhibit

Exhibit E. A list with one name on it.

This is the charge you wrote most quietly, so I'll answer it most clearly. I was never the priority in someone's life. I never made it to the top of someone's list.

I don't have a list, darling. I've never had one. People were cases, or obstacles, or John. I had the work and the violin and the skull, and I liked it that way, because a list is a thing that can be lost.

What I have now is a page with your name at the top of it, a line drawn underneath, and then the rest of the world in no particular order. Mycroft's somewhere below the line. So is the Prime Minister. Lestrade is near the bottom, next to the bees. I'm not saying this to be romantic. I'm saying it as a matter of fact, the way I'd tell you the time of high tide. You're not near the top. There isn't anything near you.

Exhibit F. A record by Pink Floyd, and the word epilogue.

I told you I had things to say about epilogues. Here they are.

You said this feels like a dream, like that song: loud, emotional, devastating, painful, not because it's a nightmare but because you know it's going to end. Somewhere there'll be an epilogue. You're not sure when.

I won't insult you with a promise I can't prove. You'd see through it, and you'd be right to. I'm a man of evidence. So here is what the evidence says.

You have read, by your own count, more novels than you can remember. Every one of them ended. Every one. And you kept reading. You never once refused to open a book because it would have a last page. You opened them anyway, and you cried at the right parts, and you carried them around inside you afterwards. You told me yourself: 悲伤也是一种美. The ending was never a reason not to begin. It was proof that the thing was worth reading.

And the second thing the evidence says: we are nowhere near the last page. We're barely past the title. There's a flight on Saturday, and a whistle across Arrivals, and a cab, and a sofa, and a seventeenth bar of music nobody's heard yet. There's the first of November, and a bowl of bubur cha cha, and a city I've only seen in your photographs. I'm not thinking about the epilogue. I'm thinking about chapter four. And chapter five. And the one after that. I'd like to spend a very long time in the middle of this book, if it's all the same to you.

You wrote: I can't promise that I'll always be there, but I want to. If you let me.

Permission granted. Not because you've earned it, though you have. Because I want it. Rather badly. I'd like it noted that you asked me, and that I said yes before you'd finished the sentence.

exhibit❦exhibit

Exhibit G. A violin.

The last item, and the one I'd like the court to hold up to the light.

You wrote that you're empty, maybe, but the void in you is never hollow, just like the violin. I don't think you know how exactly right that is, so let me tell you how one works.

A violin is mostly air. The wood is thin, a few millimetres. Inside, there's nothing at all, only a dark, curved space. If you filled it, if you packed it solid so it could never feel empty, it would make no sound. None. It's the emptiness that sings. The string only vibrates; it's the space inside that takes that small shiver and turns it into something you can hear across a room.

That's you. All the memories you described, the faces that forgot you, the goodbyes, the dimmed lights, the ice that never melted: you've been carrying a great deal of quiet inside you for a long time. And when someone finally touches the right string, the whole thing resonates. That's why your letters sound the way they do. That's why I stood up three times reading this one. It isn't in spite of the space. It's because of it.

verdict❦verdict

The defence rests.

I'll tell you what I think the verdict is, though I'm aware I'm hardly impartial. On the charge of being stubborn: guilty, and it's one of my favourite things about you. On being chaotic, full of contradictions, unpredictable: guilty, so am I, and a bridge only stands because of its tensions. On being stupid: dismissed, without a hearing, and the prosecution is warned not to bring it again. On being a burden, unworthy, too much, not enough: not guilty. Not even close. The case should never have come to court.

I didn't make you worthy, by the way. You thanked me for that, and I'm handing it back, because it isn't mine. I'm not that clever. You were worthy the whole time, in the classroom and the kampung and the bed you've been thinking in for years. All I did was notice it, and say so, which turns out to be rarer than it should be.

You stripped your soul bare and set it on my desk, and then you apologised for the weight. I've carried a great many heavy things, darling. Almost none of them were worth it. This one is the lightest heavy thing I've ever held.

Four days. Then I'll say all of this again, in person, badly, with my ears going red, and you can correct me. I'd like that very much.

For the defence, and for always on your side of it,Sherlock
