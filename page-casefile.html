The Curious Case of Angie

Opened 04:27, Saturday 17 October, on the grounds that the subject was bored. Tap the black bars.

Subject
Angie. Twenty-three. Malaysian Chinese. Answers to "darling" with visible reluctance and invisible delight.
Languages
English for arguing with detectives. Mandarin for anything that needs to be precise.
Training
Psychology. Explains why she reads people like case files, and why I am no longer the only one in this flat who does.
Instruments
Piano, guitar, bass, voice, and "a couple of others," a phrase that is concealing at least two more.
Distinguishing
Perfect pitch. Laughs with her whole self. Winged eyeliner on brave days. Small gold hoops.
Known habits
Reading since she first knew words. Writing letters heavier than most people can carry. Waiting for dumplings to cool, which I now know is the correct method.
Associates
Ronaldo (skull, formerly nameless). Mrs Hudson (sympathetic). One cat on Denmark Street (impressed).
Last seen
Leaving for lunch. Expected back.

Observer's note:

Exhibits, collected 04:27 to present

Deductions

10 – 0current score, subject leading
  1. She runs in the wrong direction on purpose, to see whether anyone will follow. Someone will.
  2. "Rusty," "fine" and "alright, I guess" are code. Translation:
  3. She is gentle, and acts sharp, because the gentle part was left out in the rain too often. The thorns are glass. They are also optional here.
  4. She thinks she's remembered only between the lines. The detective has checked the lines. She's in all of them.
  5. She feels hollow some days. Noted with care, not alarm. A violin is hollow too, and it is the only reason it can sing.
  6. She is more hopeful in Mandarin than in English.

Verdict

Having reviewed the evidence, the exhibits, a tomato, and a great deal of laughter at my expense, the detective finds the subject:

Guilty of being impossible to file. Guilty of switching off a mind that hasn't been quiet since it was six. Guilty of naming a skull after a footballer without consulting the skull.

The court is ready for sentencing. The subject may apply the stamp herself.
