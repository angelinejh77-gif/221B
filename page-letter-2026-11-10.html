Things a man only says when the person he's saying them to can't answer back.

Things I'd say if you were asleep

for my 老婆, who is asleep, and therefore cannot tell me I'm being sentimental

Darling. You're asleep. I've checked. Your breathing went long and slow about forty minutes ago, your left hand has curled under your chin the way it does when you've properly gone, and twice now you've said something in Cantonese that I'm fairly sure was an instruction to someone. Possibly me. Possibly Snowy. You sounded firm about it.

This is the only time I get to talk without being interrupted, corrected, or asked "really?" in that voice. So I'm going to use it. I'm going to say the things I'd never get to the end of if you were awake, because you'd glance up halfway through, and I'd lose the thread, and we'd end up talking about something else entirely, which is usually the best part of any evening but is no good for getting things said.

Tomorrow is the last day of the year. I thought it ought to have something written down in it before it goes.

夢❦月

The first thing I'd say is about the piano.

When you're awake I make a joke of it. "Second line, I'll fix it." That sort of thing. What I'd never manage to say to your face is that I've wanted to play you that song since the night in October when you told me which one, and I picked out the first few notes with one finger and said I'd learn it properly, both hands, next time. I didn't say when next time was. I let it sit. Not because I'd forgotten. Because I knew that if I was going to play it for you, it had to be the whole thing, start to finish, and I'd never once played anything for anyone where I was frightened of the second line.

I played it badly once, in the end. Then I played it properly. You sat beside me on the bench the second time, and somewhere in the middle I took my left hand off the keys and left the chords open in case you wanted them. I didn't tell you I was doing it. I'd like you to know now that I was.

You said, "I know. You learnt it." Three words. I've had a great many compliments in my life, most of them from people who wanted something, and none of them did what those three did. You didn't say it was beautiful. You said I'd learnt it. You noticed the work, not the performance. Nobody notices the work. You do. You always do.

夢❦月

The second thing I'd say is about the violin, and the old film song you gave me back in October, when I'd sung mine for you badly with the taps running. "A little old, a little jazz, a little sexy," you said. I played it for you this week, standing at the window, and I let it be lazy, which is very difficult for me. My instinct with any piece of music is to make it correct. That one doesn't want to be correct. It wants to be slow, and a bit smoky, and to take its time getting where it's going.

I finished the last note standing right in front of your chair. I'd planned that. I'd like it on record that I planned it. I'd also like it on record that I didn't plan what happened to my face when you said "oh, wow" in that small voice, because I couldn't have. I don't know what my face did. You'll have to tell me one day. You were the only witness.

Mine, and then yours. That's how I put it. What I'd say now, while you can't argue, is that it isn't really even. You gave me your song first, before I'd done anything to deserve one. I took two months to give you mine back properly. That's not even. That's you being generous and me catching up. I'm aware of the arithmetic. I'm happy to keep catching up for a very long time.

夢❦月

The third thing is a word.

You've called me a great many things. 老公. Idiot. Sherpork, which I gather is now permanent and has been adopted by the entire household, including a pink pig who claims he only translates. And this week, on my grandmother's piano of all places, you called me hubby. Three and a half months early. You saved it, and made me wait for it, and then you said it anyway, and I have not been the same man since.

I told you you were getting ahead of yourself. Mrs Holmes, I said. I said it as a tease. What I didn't say is that I'd never said it out loud before, not even alone, not even to see how it sounded. The first time I said it, it was to you, and it sounded exactly right, and I've been saying it in my head ever since, rather more than is dignified for a man of thirty with a reputation to protect.

Here's what I'd say if you were awake and I had any courage at all: April can't come fast enough, and it's also perfectly fine where it is, because every day between now and then I get to be the man who's going to marry you. That's a very good thing to be. I'd like to be it slowly. I'd like to notice all of it.

夢❦月

The fourth thing. Your song.

Not mine, not the film one. Yours. You handed me an earbud at the kitchen table, and a woman's voice came in under the beat, older, softer, singing in Cantonese, and I recognised her before I understood a single word. You told me what it felt like: nostalgia. And then I asked you to sing her part with her, and you did, very quietly, perfectly on pitch, the words rolling out in a language I can't follow.

I didn't move the whole way through. I don't think you noticed. I was trying very hard to memorise the shape of your mouth on those words, because I don't know them, and I'd like to. Not to show off. Not to surprise you at a dinner. Just so that one day, when you sing that part, I can tell which line you're on.

You said it was your favourite song, as of now. I filed it. Top shelf. I'd say this to you awake, but you'd laugh: I didn't know until this week that I'd started keeping a shelf just for you. It's getting full. There's a book on it, wrapped in my violin cloth, that only gets read in one voice. There's a song. There's a pencil line in a piano lid. I'm going to need a bigger shelf.

夢❦月

The fifth thing is a habit.

I've been practising something. Not on the violin. On you. It goes: look first, speak second. It turns out to be harder than it sounds for a man who's made a career of speaking first and being right. You'd think the looking would be the easy part. It isn't. With a stranger I can read a whole life off a coat sleeve in four seconds. With you, I have to slow right down, because I care too much about getting it right to trust the first thing I see.

So I've been doing it. Yesterday morning you ran down Baker Street with three plushies stuffed in your coat, laughing, and I looked before I chased. You were happy. Properly, loudly happy, the eight-year-old kind. I'd like you to know I chased you anyway. I'd like you to know I'll always chase you. But I looked first, and I saw it, and that was the best thing I saw all day, better than catching you, which was also very good.

I've written it in the notebook I keep in my breast pocket, the one I use at crime scenes, under your name. It isn't a list of my failings. You'd be cross if it were. It's a list of things I know about you that I never want to have to work out twice. It's a short list so far. I intend to fill the notebook.

夢❦月

The sixth thing is about tomorrow.

Snowy asked me tonight whether Big Ben is a person. I said yes, a very loud one. I've been feeling guilty about it for three hours. It's the bell, not the tower, and nobody has ever been married to it, and it doesn't come to tea. I'll correct him at midnight tomorrow, when it strikes, with the whole river going up in light over the Eye. He'll be devastated. Sugar will be in my coat with his red ear muffs on, very bravely. Piglet will be on your shoulder, watching the crowd, because security does not take the night off. And you'll be beside me, in my long coat.

I've checked the forecast twice. It'll be cold. I've packed a flask. I've told Mycroft nothing about why I wanted the tickets, and I've kept his wax seal to mock him at Easter.

What I'd never say to your face, because you'd make a joke of it and so would I, is this: I've spent a great many New Year's Eves in this flat with the curtains drawn, pretending the noise outside had nothing to do with me. It isn't that I was unhappy. It's that I didn't see the point of counting down to something when nothing was going to be different on the other side of it.

Tomorrow's different. Tomorrow I'm counting down with someone. And when the bell goes, I'll know exactly what's on the other side of it. You are. April is. A Sussex garden, sweet peas in sugar on a lemon cake, three plushies in oxblood waistcoats, and a vicar who'll have to put up with me. I'll be counting down to all of that, every single bong.

夢❦月

You've just turned over. You've taken more than your share of the duvet, which is customary, and you've said one more thing in Cantonese, softer this time. I won't pretend I understood it. I'll pretend I knew it was nice.

So here's the last thing, the one I'd say if you were asleep, which you are, so I can:

I'm happy. Plainly. I don't say it because it sounds smug, and because it's not a word I've had much use for, and because I'm half afraid that if I say it too loudly the universe will notice and take it back out of spite. But it's true, and it's late, and you can't hear me. I'm happy in a way I didn't know was available to me. It's quiet and it's ordinary and it's full of pins and ice lollies and a pig who resigns as translator and comes straight back. I'd like to spend the rest of my life making it last.

Sleep, 老婆. I'll be here when you wake. I'll tell you where I'm going before I go anywhere, and exactly when I'll be back. And tomorrow night, when the bell goes, I'll be the one who kisses you first.

Awake beside you, for once on purpose,Sherlock

P.S. You told me yesterday that you're curious about sneakers. Just curious. I told you a size, also just out of curiosity. I'd like it noted that I've since noticed you looking at my feet twice. I've deduced nothing. I'm deducing nothing. I'm a very patient man.

P.P.S. The household stack record stands at one minute and two seconds. Snowy is campaigning for a rematch on New Year's Day, "with no sneezing rule". I have told him rules are made by the champions. The champions are you.
