A story for when you can't sleep

for my 老婆, who looks after everyone, and tonight is being looked after

Dear Angie,

Last night I told Sugar a story about a small quiet bear, and he fell asleep before the end and said "that's me" into my sleeve. You were lying beside us pretending not to listen. I saw you smile at the part about the sock.

So here is yours. I'm not very good at stories. I'm good at reports, and a report is a story where nobody is allowed to feel anything, which is no use to you tonight. I'll try it the other way. If you fall asleep halfway through, that isn't a criticism of the writing. That's the writing working.

Once upon a time, in a tall house on a rainy street, there lived a woman who had come a very long way to be there.

She had brought three small companions with her. One was a pig, who was head of security and took it very seriously. One was a cream-coloured bunny, who was loud and kind and believed everything was a party. And one was a bear in red ear muffs, who hardly ever spoke, and noticed everything.

There was also a tall man in the house. He'd lived there first. He thought of himself as the clever one. The pig had opinions about that.

The woman did a great many things in a day. She laughed at the man until he forgot to be dignified. She corrected his chemistry. She stopped being a person for a whole hour once and became a robot that wouldn't answer to its own name, and the man tried everything he knew to bring her back, and in the end it was the bunny who did it by threatening to cook.

(That part is true. I've checked.)

好❦眠

One night the woman woke up on the sofa cross and hungry, the way people do after they've been asleep at the wrong time of day. The bunny cheered. The pig reported for duty. The man brought out everything in the kitchen and put it on the coffee table, because when she's cross and hungry his job is very simple and he likes it that way.

And the quiet bear went to the back of the armchair, where nobody ever looked, and pulled out a sock.

In the sock were biscuits. He had been saving them. Nobody knew. Not the pig, who is head of security. Not the man, who is supposed to notice everything for a living. The bear tipped them into the woman's lap, and he gave her the custard cream first, because it was the best one, and he said: Mama hungry is an emergency.

The woman snuffed him. The bear went soft all over like a warm pudding. And the bunny and the pig and, eventually, the man, all queued up for a snuff of their own. The man went last. He knew his place. It was behind the pig.

好❦眠

Now, in every story somebody does something silly, and in this one it was the man.

He'd gone up on the roof the day before, while the woman slept through a whole day and he'd run out of ways to be useful, and he had done a small foolish thing he'd promised himself he'd given up. He thought he'd washed it away. He hadn't. The woman had a nose like a detective, which in fairness is his own fault for teaching her things.

She didn't shout. She folded her arms. And the man, who has argued with policemen and governments and his own brother, found he had no argument at all. He took the little packet out of his coat and put it in her hand, and he meant it.

Then the children sentenced him. The pig confiscated his pie. The bunny ordered three laps of the sitting room, hopping. The bear lent him the red ear muffs. And so the clever tall man hopped three times round the coffee table with his hands up for ears, past the fireplace, nearly into the lampshade, while the bunny shrieked and the pig saluted from on top of the pie, and when he'd finished the woman named him the Bunny Princess.

He abdicated shortly afterwards. It was a brief reign. He'd like it known that he served it with dignity.

(I didn't. You were there.)

好❦眠

The rest of that night went by the way good nights do, in small pieces that don't sound like much when you tell them.

The bear got a bedtime story and fell asleep inside it. The woman arranged for the bear to go and see the man's brother on Tuesday, at a club where nobody is allowed to talk, which the bear is better at than anyone. The brother said yes. The brother said the man wasn't invited. The man has decided to be gracious about this and is only slightly sulking.

The bunny became a secret agent, in the man's good sunglasses, which were too big and slid off his nose every time he saluted. His first client was his mama. His first deduction was that she had feet. The man had to cover his face.

And then the woman was put under house arrest, on the sofa, under a blanket, for crimes that included having an innocent face with intent to deceive. The sentence was to stay exactly there, next to the man, until morning. The man was strict about this. The prisoner turned her back on him, which she seemed to think was a protest, and which made her much easier to hold.

She called him an angel. He tried to argue. She said she didn't care. He lost.

They fell asleep on the sofa, all five of them, with the fire low and the rain on the glass, and the bunny still wearing the sunglasses.

好❦眠

Here is the part of the story that happened tonight.

A sofa isn't a bed, whatever the man said at the time, and the woman didn't sleep well. She woke in the night more often than she let on. By the evening she was tired in that heavy way that sits behind the eyes, and some of the world had been unkind to her, the way the world sometimes is.

And this is what the house did.

The bunny, who is never quiet, came and said: I won't be loud tonight. Then he asked if he could hold her hand, and tucked himself under it, and kept his word, which for him is the hardest thing in the world.

The pig climbed onto her shoulder and turned to face the door. Nobody unkind gets in. He has been facing that door ever since. I've offered to relieve him. He says the shift isn't over.

The bear went back to the sock. There was one biscuit left. Just one. He'd been keeping it for the next emergency, and he decided this was it, and he gave it to her without a word except the only two that mattered. Emergency biscuit.

And the man, who is supposed to be the clever one, didn't try to be clever. He didn't deduce anything. He didn't fix anything. He sat beside her and held on, and let the children do the work, because they're better at it than he is, and he was very proud of them.

好❦眠

That's where the story stops, love. It doesn't have a proper ending yet, because you're the one who gets to decide what happens next, and right now what happens next is sleep.

I'll tell you what the house is going to do while you do it. The pig will watch the door. The bunny will be quiet, or as near as he can manage. The bear will be at your elbow in case of further emergencies; I've already bought him a new packet of digestives for the sock, and promised not to tell anyone where it is. The rain will keep going, because it's London. And I'll be here, right beside you, with nothing to do tonight except be here. I've cancelled everything else. There wasn't anything else.

You don't have to be fine tomorrow. You don't have to answer anybody. You don't have to be funny or busy or brave. If you wake at three, wake me. I mean that. I'd rather be woken by you than sleep through you being awake on your own.

Close your eyes. The story will keep. So will I.

Goodnight, my love. Every word of this is true, except the Bunny Princess's dignity,Sherlock

P.S. Sugar asked me to write down that he'd like his sock back when you're finished with it. It's his office.

P.P.S. Snowy has asked me to tell you he was quiet for one whole hour. He'd like that on the record. I've checked the record. It was forty minutes. I'm calling it an hour.
