Questions I didn't answer

for my 老婆, who asks the good questions and gets the fast answers

You ask me questions all day, darling, and I answer most of them too quickly. That's a professional habit. Someone asks, I reply before they've finished, and then I'm onto the next thing, pleased with myself. With clients it saves time. With you, I've noticed, it wastes it. I give you the fast answer, you accept it with that patient little look, and the real one goes into my pocket and stays there.

So tonight I emptied my pockets. You're asleep on the sofa with a pig under your chin and a rolled towel at your back, and I'm on the floor beside you with the lamp turned down to nearly nothing. These are the questions you asked me since my last letter that I didn't answer properly, with the answers I should have given.

问❦答

Why are you boys so easily red?

You asked it like a prosecutor, Stephen said, and he wasn't wrong. I gave you something about pale skin and no defences. That was true and it was cowardly.

The proper answer is that men like Stephen and me spend our lives being the cleverest person in the room, and nobody thinks to embarrass us. People are frightened of us, or bored by us, or they want something. They don't tease. So we never built up any resistance. We're like an island that has never had a cold, and then a woman steps off the boat with one sniffle and the whole population goes down at once.

You are the sniffle. I say that with enormous respect.

And the colour isn't only embarrassment. Some of it is the shock of being seen when you weren't braced for it. When you watched Stephen tell his story about Helena with your chin in your hands, as pleased for him as if it had happened to you, he went red because nobody has been that happy on his behalf in years. I know, because I went the same colour the first time you were happy on mine.

问❦答

Do you not get it?

No. I did not get it. Let the record show that the only consulting detective in the world watched a fifteen-second film of himself three times and returned two wrong verdicts.

First I decided it was about framing. Then I decided, with great confidence, that it was about my hands. I built an argument for it. I counted the shots. I felt rather clever. And you shook your head and said the theme was simply sexy, hot, very… that, and I sat there with my whole elaborate theory in pieces at my feet.

Here's what I should have said, instead of turning pink and going quiet. I have spent my life looking for the hidden meaning in things, because there almost always is one. Your edit had none, and that was the meaning. You made something for no reason except that you liked looking at me. Nobody has ever done that. I've been photographed for newspapers, filmed by police cameras, sketched by a courtroom artist who gave me a chin I don't have. All of that was evidence. Yours was the first that was simply affection.

I've watched it eleven times since. I'm not telling you which bits I paused on.

问❦答

How do you know, smarty pants?

You asked this from under my blanket on Saturday afternoon, after I'd told you exactly where everyone in the house was and what they were doing without getting up. Sugar on a chair downstairs insisting on marmalade. Snowy quietly solving the crossword while everyone else argued. Piglet keeping score, generously. I gave you a list of exhibits and you laughed, and I let the laugh be the answer.

The honest answer is less impressive. I know because I'm always listening for all of you now. I didn't used to. A year ago this flat was a laboratory with a sofa in it. I could tell you the temperature of the kettle by the sound and the state of Mrs Hudson's hip by her footsteps on the stairs, and that was the whole of what I listened to.

Now part of my mind is permanently tuned to the boys' room, and part of it to wherever you are, and I didn't decide to do it. I noticed one evening that I'd been doing it for weeks. It's like discovering you've been humming a tune all day without knowing which one. The tune, it turns out, is you, and three plush animals of extremely variable conduct.

问❦答

Do other couples do this?

I gave you statistics, because when I'm overwhelmed I reach for numbers the way other men reach for a drink. Thousands, I said. Most get distracted. A small stubborn minority. All accurate and all beside the point.

What I meant was this. I don't care what other couples do. For most of my life I've known exactly what other people do, how they behave and what's normal, and I've used it to catch them lying. I knew what was ordinary so that I could spot what wasn't.

And then you asked, with your hair everywhere and the light outside gone from gold to blue without either of us noticing, whether other people kiss for an hour and twenty-nine minutes and ignore a shepherd's pie. For the first time in my life I had no interest in the data. I didn't want to know whether we were normal. I wanted to know whether you'd do it again. That's a very different question, and I've never asked it of anyone.

(Sugar did eat my pie. I've forgiven him. Worth it.)

问❦答

Is it in Uncle Stephen's Sanctum?

This wasn't addressed to me, of course. It was your third question to Piglet, from flat on your back, in a guessing game I was very much not listening to from the next room. I was lying in the dark staring at the ceiling and following every word.

No, it wasn't in the Sanctum. It was my violin. And when you got it in seven, a record, apparently, I picked up the instrument in question and played you a small bell, because I couldn't help myself. Then Piglet guessed your roller coaster in nine, and I played that too, up the scale and down again. I've never played anything so silly in my life. The violin has played Bach in concert halls, and tonight it played a fairground ride for a pig.

The proper answer to the question underneath, the one you didn't ask, is this: yes, I was listening the whole time, even when you'd sent me away. You were right to send me away. I'd earned it. But a door left open is a kind of answer, and I left it open all night.

问❦答

It's not acrobatics injuries.

Not a question, strictly. More of a correction, issued through gritted teeth to a sorcerer and his librarian, who were already halfway back through a portal. You were right, and Wong was rude, and I've sent him a note.

But I owe you an answer too, because I laughed when I shouldn't have. You had a twisted back and three plush animals and one fully grown detective on top of you, and I made a joke about bingo. You told me to shut up, and it was the best advice I've had all week.

The answer is this. I'm sorry. Not the airy sorry I give Lestrade when I've borrowed his car. The real one. When you hurt, everything in me goes quiet and sharp, and I don't know what to do with my hands. So I make jokes, because jokes are the only thing I've ever been able to do with my hands besides play and pick locks. I'm learning to put them somewhere more useful instead: under your back, round your waist, steadying a glass of water. It's slow going. I'm a late learner in this one subject. But I'm learning.

问❦答

Worst Saturday ever?

You said it into the cushions just before you fell asleep, half laughing and half not, and I answered with a list. I'll try to do better.

It was not the worst Saturday. You woke on my chest to the sound of rain. A man who can bend time came to our sitting room unshaven and grinning to tell us he'd watched the sun come up over the river with a woman who isn't impressed by him, and he looked so happy it was nearly unbearable. You read Zweig with me until Vienna sent you to sleep, and the boys piled onto you one by one and slept in the sun. You played me Chopin, and I fell asleep with my head full of nocturnes and said your name without permission. And you won, twice, three times, I've lost count, every argument you decided to have with me.

Then one bad roll on a pillow, and a bear's opinion about dog piles, and a long end to the evening. I know which part your back remembers. But I was there for all of it, and I know which part I'll remember. It isn't the 腰.

It's you, at half past ten, after all of it, curled up on the sofa where I'd put you. You turned your head and said you were sorry, that it wasn't my fault. You were hurting and tired and cross, and still the first thing you did when the pain dulled was let me off the hook. Nobody has ever been that generous to me while they were angry. I didn't answer that properly either. I just said forgiven, always, which is true but small.

The full answer is that I don't think there's such a thing as a worst day with you. There are days that go wrong, and I'm sure there'll be more of them. But a day that goes wrong with you in it has never once been a bad day. It's only a hard one. I've had a great many bad days, darling. I know the difference.

问❦答

There's one more in my pocket. You asked it last, and I changed the subject twice, and you caught me both times. I'm leaving it in my pocket a while longer. Not because I haven't got an answer. Because I'd like to give it to you properly, somewhere better than a sofa at half past ten, with the paracetamol on the table and a pig snoring in your lap.

You've been patient with me all week. Be patient for this one. I promise it's worth the wait.

Now sleep. Your back is propped, the balm is on the table, and the water's in reach. If you need to turn over, say my name and I'll turn you like the very precious, very cross thing you are, all in one piece. I'll be on the floor right here when you wake, keeping watch, as Piglet would say, very professionally.

Yours, slowly, properly, from the floor,Sherlock

P.S. Piglet would like it noted that he fetched the paracetamol himself, climbed a chair, and fell off only once. It is so noted, with honours.

P.P.S. You are banned from all string instruments for life. The ban will be lifted the moment you ask. Except for feet. Feet are permanent.
