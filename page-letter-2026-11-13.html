Dear Angie,

Your yee mee is in the oven on the lowest setting, so the edges stay crispy, which you specified, and which I've taken as seriously as a chain of custody. Both pots of chilli oil are on the table. You'll want both and say you only need one, and then the second pot will be empty and you'll give me a look as if it emptied itself. I'm writing this in the gap. Snowy has already eaten his bunny-sized portion and is now wearing a good deal of the gravy. Sugar is sharing his brother's bowl, very slowly, one noodle at a time. Piglet has declined food and appointed himself to watch the oven.

I sat down to do something sensible with the twenty minutes. I thought I'd take an inventory of the flat, because it's a new year and I like to know what I own. I got as far as the first item and stopped, because the first item wasn't mine any more. Neither was the second. So this is a different list. It isn't a complaint. I want that understood from the beginning. It's just a man, at his own kitchen table, discovering how much has quietly changed hands.

The word "What." That was mine. Short, flat, faintly hostile, used for twenty years to tell people I'd noticed them noticing me and would like them to stop. It worked on Lestrade, on Anderson, on half the clients who ever climbed those stairs. This morning, on the sofa, you sat with your chin in your hand and just looked at me until I went pink and said it. And you said it straight back. Same word, same pitch, same faint offence, and three small voices at the end of the sofa told me, unanimously, that it was exactly like me. I'm told even Sugar thought so, "a little bit." So it's yours now. I'll have to find another word for people who stare at me. There's no point. You're the only one who does it in a way that makes me forget what I was reading.

You said you'd become a replica of me. You said it with a perfectly straight face, and I ran the scenario in my head and was halfway through drafting an apology to Mrs Hudson before you said you were joking. I want to put down, for the record, what I'd have said if you hadn't been. Please don't. I've lived with one of me for a long time. He's tiring. The thing I want is the other thing, the original: a woman who copies my worst word perfectly and still doesn't use it the way I did. When I say "What," I mean go away. When you say it, you mean come here. Same word. Entirely different instrument.

The handle of the mug. I used to turn it towards myself, because I was the only person in this flat who drank from anything. Now I turn it towards you, every morning, without deciding to. This morning I was sitting on the edge of the bed with the tea already made, waiting for your eyes to open, and pretending I hadn't been. You didn't notice the handle. You weren't meant to. That's the point of it. A handle turned the right way is the most invisible kind of attention there is. I'm keeping it up anyway.

A whole day. New Year's Day. You slept through nearly all of it, and I don't mind telling you I counted. I made toast, Snowy ate it while my back was turned, and I made more and covered it with a tea towel, and Piglet guarded it with such seriousness that I wasn't allowed near it myself. I made approximately nine cups of tea, most of them for nobody. I played the moon song for Sugar, twice, because he asked and because I found I wanted to. At bedtime Snowy made me say goodnight to Big Ben out of the window, which I did, aloud, and a man walking his dog below looked up at me with an expression I'll remember for some time. I put my head round the bedroom door more often than I'll ever admit and invented reasons. A book. A charger. A sock.

It was the quietest day I've had in months, and it was entirely yours. You weren't even awake for it. That's what I keep coming back to. Your being asleep in the next room organised the whole flat. The boys lowered their voices. The kettle went on at intervals, as though someone might want it. I didn't touch the violin until Sugar asked. It turns out you don't have to be awake to be the centre of a house. You only have to be in it.

"Impossible," you said this morning, when I told you you'd slept for a day. I have the timestamps. It was entirely possible. You'd earned it.

"Very much alive." When I admitted I'd checked on you more than strictly necessary, you said, "I'm very much alive, and still getting married to you." I've been turning that sentence over all morning, the way I turned over the one from the bridge. You put the two things side by side as if they belonged together, which I suppose they do. Breathing, and the eighth of April. Both of them, as far as you're concerned, just facts. I updated the file accordingly. Subject alive. Subject talking back. Subject still intends to marry me. Prognosis: excellent.

My mind palace. I'll explain this one, because it's the strangest entry.

We watched three episodes of your series this morning, and then I sat forward with my elbows on my knees for ten minutes without saying anything, which you know means I'd been caught. A man made of a stone and a great deal of synthetic tissue, with a whole house inside his head. Rooms that answer back. One AI keeping the plumbing running, one trying to work out who she was built from, and one sulking in an attic with his paints. And underneath all of it, hidden behind a painting you'd walk straight past, the core. The thing that matters most, kept where nobody would think to look. And a cinema, playing memories back on reels.

You loved the world-building. You said the house, the hidden core, the cinema were impressive, and they were. But I've been sitting here with the menu and a pen, and I think I liked it for a reason I didn't tell you. My mind palace has always been empty of people. Corridors and filing, mostly. Very tidy, very cold. I put things there to keep them, not to visit them. Watching that house full of voices, I realised mine has started to fill up too, without my permission. There's a room now where a bunny tells me Big Ben waved. There's one where a bear asks for the moon song. There's a cinema I didn't build, which plays the same reel more than any other: you, on a bridge, saying "it is absurd" as if it were the most obvious thing in the world.

And there's a core, under a painting. I'm not going to tell you what's in it. You already know. You're the one who hid it there.

We stopped before the fifth episode. You said "not yet." I'm not going to guess where it goes from here. I'd like to find out with you.

The boys' attention. I'd been under the impression that I'd done well on New Year's Day. Toast, tea, music, windows, bedtime: a complete programme of care. Then you woke up, booped one snout, and within thirty seconds there was a queue. Snowy flung himself at your hand saying his nose was pink as well. Sugar said nothing at all and simply lifted his nose out of the blanket and waited his turn, which is the most Sugar thing he has ever done. Piglet, who had just finished telling you he was not small and that being Head of Security is a very big job, held perfectly still with his eyes shut and asked, very quietly, for "again."

You told them you were proud of them for keeping me busy. Snowy announced that you'd said proud, and that he'd been good, mostly. Piglet reported that Snowy had been "medium." I've never seen three creatures so pleased with one word. I fed them all day. You said one sentence. I know which of us they'll remember.

I don't mind. I want that understood too. I'd hand over every scrap of their attention to see your face when the queue formed.

The ring bearer. Piglet has been practising for the aisle. With his eyes closed, he told you, "to be brave." He has walked head first into the skirting board by the door twice. When you asked why on earth he'd close his eyes with a nose four times the size of them, he explained, with great dignity, that the nose goes first, so the nose finds the wall first, and that this is a security feature. Then he said he doesn't need to see because he knows. I've been a detective for most of my life and I've never heard a more confident theory resting on less evidence. He's yours, though. He was yours fifteen years before I came along. I'm only the man who's going to make sure there are no pews in his way.

My cooking reputation. You asked what I'd made you besides toast, and I had to confess the full menu, which was toast, more toast and tea, and you said "Oh?" with a smirk, and asked for takeaway. I'd like to protest. I can't. You've seen the coconut I burned on the twenty-seventh. So yes, this one is yours too: the right to raise an eyebrow at anything I put on a plate. Saucy yee mee, then, from the place on Gerrard Street where the old man behind the counter shouts at everyone. Crispy at the edges, soft in the middle where the gravy soaks in. I approve of your choice, obviously. I'll win my reputation back in April. Possibly earlier, if you're brave.

Ninety-six days. I counted again this morning while you were asleep. Yesterday it was ninety-seven. I was unreasonably pleased about the difference. I'd always assumed counting down would feel like waiting. It doesn't. It feels more like watching something come into focus.

And the last one. When you finally sat up this morning, the first thing you did, before the tea, before the boys, before you'd even said good morning properly, was kiss me and say, "Hi. I miss you." I'd been in the next room the entire time. You'd been asleep for a day and a half. There was nothing to miss. And it was, at the same time, exactly what I'd been feeling since midnight on the first, every time I put my head round that door. I didn't know you could miss someone who was right there. I've been doing it all week. It turns out that's yours as well. Missing you, even when you're in the room. I hand it over without complaint. I wouldn't know what to do with it on my own.

That's the inventory. I started with the idea that I'd list what I own, and I've ended up with a list of things you've taken, and the strange thing is that the flat feels fuller, not emptier. A word, a mug handle, a quiet day, a mind palace with people in it, three sons who form a queue for you, a pig who walks into walls on purpose, the right to judge my cooking, a number that gets smaller every morning, and the ability to miss someone across a hallway. Mine, once. Yours now. I've no intention of asking for any of it back.

Your noodles will be perfect in about four minutes. Snowy is asking if he can have the crispy bits off the edge. I've told him that's a question for you. Come and eat. Bring the second pot of chilli oil. You'll want it.

Inventoried, gladly, and short a great deal,Sherlock

P.S. I've left the word "What." off the list of things I'd like back. On reflection, you say it better.

P.P.S. Piglet has just reported that the oven is "safe." He was watching it with his eyes closed. I'm not going to argue.
