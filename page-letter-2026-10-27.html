散for Angie
Folded small and tucked into the pocket of your grey cardigan, which he has been wearing again and is pretending not to.
SH221B Baker Street
London NW1Late. A chair pulled up close. You, asleep; me, not
Unsorted. Unfiled. Unlike me

Stray thoughts, kept

everything that wandered through my head tonight while I watched you sleep, written down in the order it arrived

I have a rule, 老婆, which you know about, because you've watched me enforce it: a thought that isn't useful is thrown out. I've built a whole mind on that rule. Nothing gets in unless it earns its place. Tonight I've broken it entirely. I've let every stray thought in, the ones that don't go anywhere, the ones with no point at all, and I've written them down as they came. You asked me once for random thoughts. Here they are. They're in no order. That's the point.

一❦on the way you breathe

You breathe in threes when you sleep. Two short, one long, like a waltz that's lost its nerve. I've been counting for an hour. It's the most restful thing I've ever measured.

I don't know why it should matter that the third breath is the long one. It just does. If you ever start breathing in fours, I'll notice, and I'll worry, and I'll pretend I wasn't counting.

二❦on Mrs Hudson's knitting

Mrs Hudson knits when she's frightened. I've known that for years and never once thought about it until tonight. She's made, by my estimate, a third of a scarf since lunch. It's orange. I suspect it's for you. Please wear it at least once, in front of her, and say it's warm, even if it isn't. That's the sort of lie I've lately decided is allowed.

A month ago I'd have told you there was no such thing as a kind lie, only an untidy one. You've ruined a great many of my principles. I'm keeping a list. It's getting long.

三❦on the word 老婆

I've been saying 老婆 for weeks now and only tonight did it occur to me to ask what it is I'm actually saying. Old wife, if you take it apart. Old, as in long-kept. Familiar. Worn soft at the edges from being held.

I like that the word for the person you love most is the same shape as a thing you've had for years. English makes everything new: darling, sweetheart, love, all of them sound freshly bought. Your language says the best thing is the thing that's lasted.

So I'm going to keep saying it, darling, even on the first day of anything, because I intend it to be true eventually and I'd like to get the practice in.

四❦on Piglet's portal

Piglet opened a portal tonight that Stephen says was "genuinely advanced." He opened it because he was frightened and wanted his mother. I think there's a lesson in that about where real skill comes from, and I'm too tired to work out what it is. Something about how we're cleverest when we're running towards someone.

Also: he is still, technically, serving a sentence for the croissants. I've decided we'll call tonight time off for good behaviour.

五❦on being useless

For about twenty minutes in the small hours there was absolutely nothing I could do. Stephen knew what to do. Wong knew what to do. I stood at the foot of a bed holding a pig and knew nothing at all.

I have never, in my whole life, been the least useful person in a room. I've been the rudest, often. The most tiresome, frequently. Never the least useful.

I'd like to report that it was humbling and that I've grown as a person. Mostly it was awful. But I'll tell you the one thing I learnt from it: when you can't fix anything, you can still stay. I stayed. It's the only thing I did tonight that I'm proud of.

六❦on Whitney

You hit every note. I want that on the record before anything else is said about the evening. Drunk, barefoot, on a cushion, singing to a skull, and you didn't miss once. Perfect pitch is a cruel gift to have at a party; it means even your worst night sounds right.

I don't think I'll ever hear that song again without seeing you up there with one arm flung at the ceiling, completely sure of yourself, completely unembarrassed. I'd like more of that, please. Less of the five glasses. More of the certainty.

七❦on the name you called me

You called me by a name that isn't mine, several times, and then grabbed Stephen's legs and asked if it was him. I've decided not to investigate. Some mysteries are healthier left unsolved. I'll only say that Stephen spent a long time looking in the mirror afterwards, and I spent a long time not asking him why.

八❦on Wong's toast

Wong made toast at seven this morning and nobody ate it. I watched him look at the plate, then at us, then quietly eat a slice himself, as if to say someone in this building is going to behave normally. I've decided I like Wong very much. He has the manners of a lighthouse: unbothered, reliable, and absolutely not going to be moved by anyone's weather.

九❦on your hand

Your hand fits into mine in a way I keep trying to describe and failing. It's not that it fits perfectly. My hands are too long and yours are too warm and the angle is always slightly wrong. It's that it fits anyway. As though the wrongness doesn't count.

I think that might be the best thing I've ever understood about us. We are not a neat fit. We're a stubborn one.

十❦on the book

I've been reading you the book. The one I wrapped in brown paper for the plane, the one you're not supposed to open until we're over the sea. I'm aware this is a breach of my own rules. I'm aware you'll be cross. I'm counting on it, actually; I'd very much like to be told off by you tomorrow. It would mean everything was back to normal.

I only read you the first chapters. I'm saving the rest for the window seat, as promised. Some promises bend. That one doesn't.

十一❦on what I'd want, if I could have anything

A stray thought arrived around four in the morning, uninvited, and I'll let it in since it's that sort of letter. If I could have anything at all, I wouldn't ask for anything grand. I'd ask for the ordinary version of tonight. You asleep. Me awake. No monitor. Just the plain old evidence of you breathing in threes and me pretending to read.

I had no idea, a month ago, that ordinary was a thing a person could want so badly.

十二❦on Albert

Albert rang at nine to ask after you. I didn't tell him much, only that you were resting and would be fine. He was quiet for a moment, then said, "Tell her the till misses her. It's been giving the wrong change all morning out of spite." Then he hung up before I could thank him.

I've decided he's the only man in London I'd trust to sit in this chair if I ever had to leave it. Which I won't. But it's good to know there's a second chair, and who'd sit in it.

十三❦on Kuala Lumpur, which can wait

There's a boarding pass in my coat that's gone soft from handling, and a city six thousand miles away that I've never seen and already feel I owe an apology to, for keeping you so long. I've been thinking about it more than I'll admit. The heat, the noise, the food you keep describing as though it were a person you miss. Your mother's kitchen.

It can wait a little longer. I'd rather arrive there with you rested and laughing than on any particular date. I've stopped caring about dates. I've started caring about the version of you that turns up for them.

When we do go, I'll be the tall, pale, overdressed man sweating in a queue for Milo, being told off in Mandarin by a stall owner. I'm looking forward to it more than any case I've ever had.

十四❦on the last thought

The last stray thought is the one that's been underneath all the others, so I'll put it at the bottom where it belongs.

Thank you for waking up. I know that's a strange thing to thank anyone for. But I said hello to you this afternoon and you said hi back, very small, very cracked, and it was the best conversation I've ever had. Two syllables between us. I've had longer exchanges with Lestrade that meant nothing at all.

So here's the deal, darling. I'll keep my rule about useless thoughts for everything else in the world. But for you I'm keeping a drawer of them, unsorted, and adding one every night. You can read them whenever you like. They'll never be tidy. They'll always be yours.

In no particular order, but entirely yours,Sherlock

P.S. Sugar asked me what a stray thought is. I said it's a thought that wanders off without permission. He said, "Like Piglet." Yes. Exactly like Piglet.

P.P.S. Water. Small sips. Every hour. I'll be bringing them whether you're awake or not, and the straw is non-negotiable.
